# GENESIS-2.22 — Seed-Invariant Generalization

## Hypothesis

Test whether a predictor trained on two GENESIS seeds can generalize to a third unseen seed without refitting.

The experiment is measurement-only. The universe rules, observer, memory, and target dynamics are unchanged. Coherence history is not used as an input feature.

## Protocol

- seeds: 390001, 390002, 390003
- target protocol: leave-one-seed-out
- training: two seeds
- target: one unseen seed
- ticks per seed: 2,000
- history lengths: 2, 3, 5
- chronological holdout
- exact consecutive ticks
- zero-change baseline
- deterministic shuffled control
- normalized transferred representation
- fail-closed decision: every tested case must beat both zero-change and shuffled controls

## Interpretation

GENESIS-2.22 does not establish a seed-invariant predictive representation.

The normalized predictor generally contains ordered information relative to the shuffled control, but this information does not transfer accurately enough to beat the zero-change baseline on any held-out target seed.

This strengthens the conclusions of GENESIS-2.20 and the subsequent state-transfer experiments:

1. isolated within-seed predictive structure exists in some observer representations;
2. the tested representations do not generalize robustly across seeds;
3. beating a shuffled control is insufficient evidence of predictive usefulness;
4. the primary criterion remains held-out improvement over the zero-change baseline;
5. isolated positive cases must remain hypotheses rather than being promoted to mechanisms.

No prediction is fed back into `GenesisUniverse`. No goals, rewards, agency, self-model, or endogenous learning are introduced.

## Research status

The seed-invariant generalization hypothesis is **closed as negative under the tested protocol**.

The next research step should therefore focus on identifying whether any predictive representation is invariant at the level of the underlying universe rule rather than at the level of a seed-specific observer coordinate system. A future positive claim should require explicit cross-seed generalization and held-out baseline superiority.
